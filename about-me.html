What Makes Me Different?

I help professionals turn their career aspirations into intentional, meaningful career paths—and discover their North Star along the way. With nearly three decades of experience in Nevada and a background spanning journalism, communications, public policy, human resources, talent development, and organizational leadership, I've dedicated my career to helping people recognize their potential and take bold, courageous steps toward what's next. I've designed and led career development and internal mobility programs that have helped hundreds of employees build new skills, pursue opportunities, and advance their careers. As someone who has earned a bachelor's degree, master's degree, and doctorate, I believe deeply in lifelong learning, especially in a constantly changing professional landscape.

My approach to career management is inspired by the policymaking process. Just as effective policy moves from identifying a need to creating, implementing, and evaluating a solution, your career requires the same level of intention:

1. Identify — Define Your Career Agenda

What problem are you trying to solve? Are you looking for a new role, greater fulfillment, advancement, a career change, or simply clarity about what comes next? We identify where you are, where you want to go, and what matters most to you: your career North Star.

2. Formulate — Build Your Strategy

Once we understand your goals, we develop a strategy to get there. This may include identifying your strengths, closing skill gaps, exploring career paths, building relationships, finding mentors, or creating a development plan.

3. Adopt — Define and Strengthen Your Brand

A great career strategy only works when you can communicate your value. We clarify your professional identity and position your experience, expertise, and aspirations through your résumé, LinkedIn profile, networking presence, and personal brand.

4. Implement — Put Your Career Strategy Into Action

This is where intention becomes action. We put your tools and strategy to work—whether that means applying for roles, having career conversations, networking, pursuing education, seeking stretch assignments, or positioning yourself for advancement.

5. Evaluate — Evolve With the Landscape

Your career is never a finished policy. We continually assess what is working, what has changed, and what skills and experiences the market demands. Then we adjust your strategy so you remain relevant, competitive, and aligned with your goals.

Through my career consultation business, I bring this same intentionality to individuals who are ready to gain clarity, build confidence, and take ownership of what comes next. I want people to see their careers as more than a job or a title. Your greatest work may still be ahead of you, and there is so much more to explore.
